# Yemeni wedding customs and event sequence (men and women, by region)

Research date: 2026-10-19. Most usable sources are 2019-2025 Arabic news features. Several regional sources (Tihama, Hadramawt) are older (1999-2007) and are flagged as such. Several key pages (Al-Masdar Online on Aden, 14 October on Hadramawt) returned HTTP 403 and could only be seen as search snippets.

## 1. Sequence of events, who organises and pays, days and times

### Takeaway
A northern/Sanaani wedding is two separate, parallel tracks. The men's wedding is short: usually 2 days, henna/سمرة on Wednesday night, then on Thursday the برع dance, lunch, the afternoon qat مقيل and the night زفة. The women's wedding runs about a week of named days, from الحمام/الذبال through النقش and الحفلة to الدخلة, then post-wedding visits (الثالث، الشكمة، السابع). The groom's family carries most of the cost. Before any of this come the contract (عقد/ملكة, written by the الأمين الشرعي or مأذون) and the mahr/gold (المهر والشرط) negotiation.

### Cited Findings
**Pre-wedding stages**
- Tihama (1999, old): the groom's father, not the son, chooses the family and checks its standing. A go-between called *المنسوب* (Almansoub) carries the proposal, and the bride's father answers after about two days. The groom's father then negotiates *المهر والشرط* with the bride's father. The *مأذون* writes the contract at the bride's father's house, and the wedding date is fixed at that event while women ululate — [Yemen Times 1999](https://yementimes.com/marriage-traditions-in-tihama-archives1999-24-culture-4/)
- The betrothal visit: the groom and close male relatives bring raisins, qat and other gifts to the bride's father (undated, generic source) — [Zawaj.com](https://www.zawaj.com/weddingways/yemeni.html)
- Aden and the south: a multi-day sequence of *يوم الخطبة* → *يوم العقد* → *يوم الزَّقْرة والغُسل* → *ليلة الحناء* → *يوم العرس*. The bride often wears a traditional green dress (2025) — [Dar Al-Ilham](https://drelhamonline.com/yemen-adeni-ritual-alqahra-day/)
- Contract officials: trustees (*الأمناء الشرعيين*) enforce the 2021 Houthi "تيسير الزواج" marriage-cost rules, which confirms that the الأمين الشرعي is the contract official in the north — [Al-Araby Al-Jadeed 2021](https://www.alaraby.co.uk/society/%D8%BA%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%87%D9%88%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%B1%D8%A8-%D9%88%D8%A3%D8%B2%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%A7-%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%B5%D8%B1-%D8%B9%D8%B2%D9%91%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%86)

**Mahr, الشرط and gold (2021 figures)**
- Pre-war custom: a cash mahr of up to "مليون ريال" (about $4,000) went to the bride's father, and the groom also paid for the wedding and the jewellery. The Houthi document fixed the cash mahr at about $1,300 for a virgin bride and half that for a widow, with a deferred mahr (*المهر الآجل*) of 4 gold pounds payable on divorce. Mahr in Sanaa is about $1,600. In Taiz, one father demanded 40 g of gold. A gold pound rose from about 60,000 YER to about 399,000 YER (~$1,600). One groom put jewellery alone at about 2 million YER (~$8,000). Some Taiz families have dropped the cash mahr and put all wedding costs on the groom — [Al-Araby Al-Jadeed 2021](https://www.alaraby.co.uk/society/%D8%BA%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%87%D9%88%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%B1%D8%A8-%D9%88%D8%A3%D8%B2%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%A7-%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%B5%D8%B1-%D8%B9%D8%B2%D9%91%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%86)
- The groom pays for jewellery, the hall and its supplies (*صالة الزفاف ومستلزماتها*), clothing, and the new apartment's furniture — same source.
- North vs south (2004, old): in the north the groom pays the dowry, furnishes the house and funds the banquet. In the south the bride brings household items (bedroom furniture, kitchenware). A Sanaa wedding then cost about 1 million YER — [Yemen Times 2004](https://yementimes.com/weddings-in-yementraditions-and-social-customs-of-marriage-archives2004-733-culture/)

**Sanaa men's wedding**
- Thursday is the preferred wedding day, and Wednesday night is the henna night and evening gathering. Relatives put henna on the groom's hands and feet while singing, and young men compete for the leftover henna. On Thursday morning, songs are played over loudspeakers and guests dance the *برع* with janbiyas to the *المرافع* drums. Lunch follows: *بنت الصحن، الشفوت، الفحسة، المرق*, and meat with rice. Then comes the afternoon qat majlis, which opens with *زفة الديوان* (a munshid's nasheed blessing the groom), and oud players perform until the "ساعة سليمانية" before sunset. At the night *زفة* the groom wears jasmine garlands and a crown of herbs and flowers, carries a sword or rifle, and is lifted on shoulders (2021) — [Al-Mushahid 2021](https://almushahid.net/75127/)
- The men's wedding is 2 days, "الحناء والدخلة". It takes place in the groom's street or neighbourhood, which is decorated. Morning: *البرع* with *الطاسة* and *المرفع*. Afternoon: lunch, chanting and oud. Night: *السمرة* (mizmar and songs until dawn). On the second day the السمرة ends at 10 PM and is followed by the groom's *زفة* to his bride. Summer is the wedding season (2019) — [Al-Ain 2019](https://al-ain.com/article/sanaa-wedding-houthi-militia)
- Older detail (2004): the groom and guests may visit a bathhouse early on the wedding day, followed by breakfast and drumming around the neighbourhood. The *المقيل* runs from after lunch until about 7 PM. The *زفة* lasts 1.5 to 2 hours. Dancing goes on from 10 PM to dawn. Guests give money to the groom (*الطرح*) on henna night or after the procession. Neighbours help bake through *الغُرم* — [Yemen Times 2004](https://yementimes.com/weddings-in-yementraditions-and-social-customs-of-marriage-archives2004-733-culture/)

**Sanaa women's wedding (about one week)**
- 2021 list: *التوديع* (girls bid farewell to the bride's single life) → *الحمام* (steam bath, then a procession home and a singing party) → *الذبال* (organised by the groom's relatives; women wear traditional Sanaani dress; a female singer drums on a صحن and spoon) → *النقش* (henna/dye by the bride's mother and sisters) → *الحفلة* (a women-only party in a hall) → *النسمة* (the bride is decorated again and rests) → *الدخلة* (white dress; she is escorted to her husband) — [Al-Mushahid 2021](https://almushahid.net/75127/)
- 2019 list: *الحمام → الذبال* (the groom's family provides the bride's traditional outfits and hires a female folk singer for the full day) → *النقش → الحفلة* (in a women's hall) → *النسمة → الدخلة*. The bride wears a different dress each day. The groom's family bears most of the cost and the bride's family contributes little — [Al-Ain 2019](https://al-ain.com/article/sanaa-wedding-houthi-militia)
- After the wedding (2019 interview with folk singer تقية يحيى سند): *الذبال* (the bride's hammam outing, sometimes in the *القرقوش* headdress) → *المشاجب* → *النقش* → the official day → the bride's departure, usually on Thursday → *الثالث* (at the groom's house, hosted by his family, with a zaffa for the bride) → *الشكمة* (hosted by the bride's mother at the bride's family home; the bride does no housework until after it) → *اليوم السابع* (the bride's whole family visits the husband's house). Weddings once lasted a month and now last about a week — [Manshoor 2019](https://manshoor.com/arts-and-culture/sanaa-wedding-changes/)
- The morning after, the groom visits his parents, kisses their heads and receives well-wishers (search snippet of Sanaa features, 2019-2021) — [Al-Ain 2019](https://al-ain.com/article/sanaa-wedding-houthi-militia)

**Hadramawt and Tihama**: see section 2.

### Inferences
- An app should model a wedding as two linked timelines, men and women, each with several named days. It should not assume a single "wedding day". The anchor is Thursday (الدخلة/الزفة), with Wednesday evening for henna/السمرة. Women's days usually start 4 to 6 days earlier.
- Time slots that recur in the sources are morning (برع), lunch (غداء, at the groom's family), afternoon until maghrib (مقيل/qat), and evening after isha (سمرة/زفة). Women's hall events in Houthi areas must end by 8 PM (see section 4).
- Each event can have a different host: the groom's family hosts the الذبال, the lunch, the men's events and الثالث, and the bride's mother hosts الشكمة. Payer and host should be per-event fields.

### Gaps
- No source found that describes *الشوفة* (the viewing visit), the *شبكة* (the term does not appear in the Yemeni sources found; *الشرط/الذهب* is used instead), *التكشيرة*, or the *الصبحية* in Sanaa specifically. *الملكة* as a separate contract-day celebration was not documented either. The question's naming may follow other Gulf/Levant usage, so it needs validation with Yemeni users.
- No 2022-2026 itemised cost figures were found. The "$10k-20k" estimate (search snippet from Al-Ain citing منظمة الأسرة السعيدة) is undated — [Al-Ain](https://al-ain.com/article/wedding-dress-marriage-yemen)

## 2. Differences by region (local names)

### Takeaway
The Sanaa pattern is the best documented. Aden and the south use *يوم الزقرة والغسل* and *ليلة الحناء*, and the bride brings the furnishings. Hadramawt has its own vocabulary: *الدهينة* (the first wedding night, under the groom's family house), *العراض*, *الشرح* (songs with drums), *الشراع*, and a *صبحية* the morning after the wedding night. Tihama (Hodeidah) uses a large straw tent, *المخدرة*, outside the house, with qat until past midnight and then the zaffa. No good sources were found for Taiz, Ibb, Dhamar or Mukalla beyond the mahr and cost data.

### Cited Findings
- **Sanaa**: see section 1 (Al-Mushahid 2021, Al-Ain 2019, Manshoor 2019).
- **Aden/Lahj**: *يوم الزَّقْرة والغُسل* falls a day or more before the wedding. The bride stays secluded in a private room at her family's home, and only her closest female relatives prepare her: henna, hair, and *الهِرد، الزباد، الطيب*. In parts of Lahj a *الماشطة* dresses the bride while the other women dance in another room. The bride wears green (2025) — [Dar Al-Ilham](https://drelhamonline.com/yemen-adeni-ritual-alqahra-day/)
- **Aden**: the groom's family holds a gathering called *المخدرة* several weeks before the wedding night (search snippet only; the page returned 403) — [Al-Masdar Online](https://almasdaronline.com/article/14032). Aden's outskirts (older): the groom's procession on the wedding night features traditional dress and a sword, and the bride's procession answers the groom's with one gunshot — [Marebpress](https://marebpress.net/articles.php?id=8551)
- ***يوم الدروع***: a newer event named after the Adeni women's traditional *درع* (a wide embroidered chiffon gown). It is now borrowed into Sanaa weddings alongside bachelorette and themed-dress nights — [Manshoor 2019](https://manshoor.com/arts-and-culture/sanaa-wedding-changes/)
- **Hadramawt, Wadi (undated forum/encyclopedia, likely older)**: henna night is called *الدهينة* and is the first actual wedding rite, held in a wide space below the groom's family house. Four specialist women (*المحنيات*), two for the hands and two for the feet, do the bride's henna and naqsh. *العراض* is a folk welcome with men's games for notable guests — [Hawamer](https://hawamer.com/vb/hawamer663568); [Arabpage](https://arabpage.net/3351/)
- **Hadramawt, Ghayl Ba Wazir (2007, old)**: the henna evening (*الحنه*) is at the groom's house, with men's folk games until late. The groom is hennaed among the women. The next morning a *الشراع* (awning) goes up under or on top of the bride's house, with *الشرح* (singing with drums) (search snippet) — [Hadhramaut.info](https://www.hadhramaut.info/view/9157.aspx)
- ***الصبحية* (Hadramawt, al-Dulay'a)**: the morning after the wedding night, women wait at the groom's door and ululate when he comes out. Then the *المبيتات* (women who stayed overnight) may enter the bride's room. The groom's female relatives welcome the bride with a special ululation used only for this occasion (search snippet) — [Yemeress/al-Gomhoriah](https://www.yemeress.com/algomhoriah/2099958)
- **Hadramawt, Shibam (2004, old)**: invitations (*وكاد*) are carried 3 days ahead. A *قهوة* family meeting is held 8 days before. The dowry is called *قباز* and is shown to witnesses in a box. On *ليلة الحكي* the bride bathes and is hennaed. *المشتى* follows the next day (bath, coffee, henna, guests bring gifts/*الطرح*). Women gather in a separate room or divan — [Yemen Times 2004](https://yementimes.com/weddings-in-yementraditions-and-social-customs-of-marriage-archives2004-733-culture/)
- **Tihama/Hodeidah (1999, old)**: a wood-and-straw tent, *المخدرة*, lit with coloured lights, is set up outside the bride's house. In the afternoon guests eat full meat meals, chew qat on wooden beds (سرر) and straw mats, and listen to *منشدين* with *القصّابين* (flute and drums). The gathering lasts past midnight and then the *الزفة* begins. Notable groom parties fire guns. Traditionally the bride left on a camel. Prestige depends on the number of cattle slaughtered — [Yemen Times 1999](https://yementimes.com/marriage-traditions-in-tihama-archives1999-24-culture-4/)
- **Marib (tribal)**: *الشرح* is a women's evening of drumming and dance at the bride's mother's house before the wedding, and gunfire announces the groom's procession (search snippet) — [Al-Quds Al-Arabi](https://www.alquds.co.uk/اعراس-قبائل-مأرب-اليمنية-اهازيج-شعبية/)
- **Al-Mahra**: a dedicated article exists on its own wedding customs and was not fetched — [Al-Mahriah](https://almahriah.net/culture/219)
- **Ibb, Taiz, Dhamar**: only cost and restriction data was found. The 2021 Houthi mahr document's enforcement began in Ibb, with the first wedding under it on 18 June 2021 in al-Dhihar district — [Al-Araby 2021](https://www.alaraby.co.uk/society/%D8%BA%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%87%D9%88%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%B1%D8%A8-%D9%88%D8%A3%D8%B2%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%A7-%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%B5%D8%B1-%D8%B9%D8%B2%D9%91%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%86). A Dhamar tribal pact bans women from smartphones and the internet — [More to Her Story](https://www.moretoherstory.com/stories/in-yemen-tribal-decrees-are-tightening-their-grip-on-women-as-inequality-deepens)

### Inferences
- Event names should be configurable per region rather than one fixed list. *المخدرة* means a Tihama tent in one source and an Adeni groom's gathering in another. *الشرح* is Hadrami drum singing but a Marib women's evening. *الذبال* means different things in the two Sanaa sources.
- Who hosts which side differs: the Tihama tent stands outside the bride's house, while the Hadrami الدهينة is under the groom's house.

### Gaps
- No Mukalla-specific (coastal Hadramawt city) or current (2020s) Hadrami description was fetched. Modern Taiz/Ibb/Dhamar event sequences were not found, and neither was any current Tihama source. These need local interviews or more Yemeni sources (عدن الغد، المصدر أونلاين, YouTube channels).

## 3. Men's wedding vs women's wedding: places, attendees, entertainment, food, qat

### Takeaway
Men celebrate in public or semi-public spaces: the street or neighbourhood, a diwan/majlis, and historically tents. Their programme is البرع, lunch, the qat مقيل with munshid and oud, then the night زفة. Women celebrate in homes, the hammam and, increasingly, women-only halls (صالات نسائية), with female singers (مزاينة/فنانة) and more recently DJs. The bride's family and the groom's female relatives both take part.

### Cited Findings
- Men: the venue is the groom's street, decorated. Food is a lunch for the groom and guests. Entertainment is البرع with الطاسة/المرفع and janbiyas, chanting and oud, and mizmar in the السمرة — [Al-Ain 2019](https://al-ain.com/article/sanaa-wedding-houthi-militia)
- Men's qat majlis: it opens with *زفة الديوان*, and oud runs until before sunset. Tents were used for the majlis until the mid-1990s, and halls became common afterwards — [Al-Mushahid 2021](https://almushahid.net/75127/)
- Women: hammam outing, female singer with صحن and spoon, henna by the mother and sisters, and a women-only hall party — [Al-Mushahid 2021](https://almushahid.net/75127/). Historically zaffa singing and drumming were done by women from hereditary performer families (*المزاينة*). DJs with loudspeakers now dominate, and foreign/Arab songs have replaced folk songs — [Manshoor 2019](https://manshoor.com/arts-and-culture/sanaa-wedding-changes/)
- The groom traditionally comes to the women's hall to collect the bride (implied by the 2024 Houthi ban on him entering to do so) — [YDN 2024](https://ydn.news/?p=60470)

### Inferences
- Guest lists, venues and vendors (female singer, DJ, munshid, oud player, cook) should be per-track. Qat is a men's-majlis line item. Food is mainly the men's lunch hosted by the groom's family.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies typical guest counts or the qat budget for the 2020s, and none says whether women chew qat at women's wedding days.

## 4. Privacy norms at women's events

### Takeaway
Women's events are strictly closed to men. In Houthi-controlled areas, a 2024 directive in Hamdan (Sanaa governorate) banned photographing the bride (photo or video) inside halls, banned the groom from entering, and set an 8 PM end time. Separately, tribal pacts restrict women's smartphones. Strong cultural demand for female photographers is documented. No Yemeni source documents phone-covering or how photos are shared afterwards.

### Cited Findings
- Hamdan directive (October 2024): women's hall events must end by 8 PM. Singing, loudspeakers, opening hall windows and DJs in women's parties are banned. The groom may not enter the hall to collect the bride or take photos with her. Photographing or filming the bride inside halls is banned. Hall owners and the bride's family face fines up to 500,000 YER (~$1,000) — [YDN 2024](https://ydn.news/?p=60470); [Barran Press](https://barran.press/news/5832); [Al-Mashhad Al-Yemeni](https://www.almashhad.news/news/293121)
- Since 2019, more than 70 restrictive tribal "marriage pacts" in Houthi areas have banned women from singing and carrying smartphones. In Amran, songs, keyboards, DJs and loudspeakers are banned at weddings in halls and homes — [More to Her Story](https://www.moretoherstory.com/stories/in-yemen-tribal-decrees-are-tightening-their-grip-on-women-as-inequality-deepens)
- Female photographers: Sanaa photographer Eman Tuhamah photographed women's wedding ceremonies, despite initial family opposition (2018) — [The New Arab 2018](https://alaraby.co.uk/english/indepth/2018/4/9/yemeni-women-photographers-strive-to-overcome-barriers). In Mahweet, brides need a female photographer because it is not acceptable for a man to photograph them — [Mercy Corps](https://www.mercycorps.org/blog/yemen-community-photography)
- Regional (not confirmed for Yemen, 2026): at women's weddings guests' phones are covered with stickers, and only the hired documentation team or influencer may shoot and publish — [25h.app 2026](https://25h.app/2026/07/21/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%A4%D8%AB%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%B6%D9%8A%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A3%D8%B9%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B3-%D9%86%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%A6/)

### Inferences
- An app should default women's events to no men, no public photo sharing, female-only vendors (photographer, singer/DJ) and restricted visibility of guest lists and images. Rules depend on the authority: Houthi-area rules are stricter than in government areas.

### Gaps
- No Yemeni source found on phone check-in or sticker practice at halls, or on how brides share photos afterwards (e.g. private WhatsApp only). Women's hall rules in Aden, Taiz and Mukalla were not found.

## 5. Recent changes: halls, mass weddings, war and economy, expatriates

### Takeaway
Halls replaced tents and homes from the late 1990s onward. The war pushed some couples back to homes or small gatherings, raised hall rents (~$200 → ~$600), and shortened celebrations from a month to about a week. Gold prices in YER have multiplied. Gulf-funded mass weddings (أعراس جماعية) of hundreds of couples became common. Houthi authorities impose mahr caps, music bans and levies.

### Cited Findings
- Hall rent rose from ~$200 before the war to ~$600. Airstrikes on venues pushed some couples to homes and small gatherings, and others chose group weddings or postponed. Celebrations shrank from a month to about a week (2019) — [Manshoor 2019](https://manshoor.com/arts-and-culture/sanaa-wedding-changes/)
- Hall owners attribute rising rents to Houthi levies "for the war effort". Houthi preachers and *زوامل* take over wedding microphones, invitations are pressured to carry the Houthi logo, and singing is banned in some neighbourhoods — [Al-Mushahid 2021](https://almushahid.net/75127/)
- The 2021 Houthi "تيسير الزواج" rules cap mahr, ban singing, folk dancing and loudspeakers, and set penalties of 2 months in prison and a fine — [Al-Araby 2021](https://www.alaraby.co.uk/society/%D8%BA%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%87%D9%88%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%B1%D8%A8-%D9%88%D8%A3%D8%B2%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%A7-%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%B5%D8%B1-%D8%B9%D8%B2%D9%91%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%86)
- Mass weddings: the Emirates Red Crescent held its 14th Yemen mass wedding, the 4th in Mukalla, for 200 young people (2019) — [Emarat Al-Youm 2019](https://www.emaratalyoum.com/politics/issues/yemen-latest/2019-10-28-1.1267046). An ERC-supported wedding of 500 in Mokha — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/yemen/erc-supports-wedding-of-500-young-yemenis-in-mokha-1.500365659). Mass weddings as "a flower amid the war" — [Al-Estiklal](https://www.alestiklal.net/ar/article/dep-news-1566584864)
- Bridal dress rental can reach ~$200 per 24 hours, and a symbolic-price dress initiative exists (Al-Ain, search snippet) — [Al-Ain](https://al-ain.com/article/wedding-dress-marriage-yemen)
- War displacement and postponement: one groom postponed his wedding twice because of the war — [Al-Ain 2019](https://al-ain.com/article/sanaa-wedding-houthi-militia)

### Inferences
- Halls (separate men's and women's, or women-only) are now the main venue type in cities. Mass weddings are an event type with a sponsor, not a family host.

### Gaps
- No source quantified expatriate grooms (Saudi/Gulf/US) or their effect on timing (e.g. compressed weddings during home leave) or mahr. The only expatriate mention is an Ibb merchant in the US commenting on mahr rules (Al-Araby 2021). No 2023-2026 cost survey was found. Mass-wedding data from after 2020 was not found.
