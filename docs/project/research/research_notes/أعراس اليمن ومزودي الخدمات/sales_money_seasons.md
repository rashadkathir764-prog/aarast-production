# Yemeni weddings: sales vendors, money, currency, payments and seasons

Research date: 2026-10-19. The web search tool returned few results in Arabic and almost no primary pages for shops: Facebook, Instagram and TikTok pages mostly could not be fetched. Many details on how vendors work are therefore listed under Gaps instead of being filled in from memory.

## 1. Sales vendors (groom clothing, bride dresses, gold, qat, sweets, water, invitations): what they sell and how people order

### Takeaway
Kerbas (كرباس - Kerbas) is a men's clothing and tailoring store in Sana'a on Shari' al-Khamsin. It sells thobes, coats and wool shawls, and its page also calls it an events contractor. Bride dresses are commonly **rented** in both Sana'a and Aden. Rental prices are posted in YER, and some ads quote SAR. Shops take deposits, sometimes in gold, and some offer free delivery and pickup. Shops sell mainly through Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and OpenSooq listings. I found no reliable sources on how qat, sweets, water or invitation-card vendors take orders.

- An older source says the groom traditionally must buy "enough qat for all in attendance". — [Yale Globalist](https://globalist.yale.edu/politics-and-economy/in-yemen-the-more-the-merrier/)
- **Sweets and food (Aden, 2019):** 400 meals with drinks cost 170,000 YER, and 150 cake trays with drinks 60,000 YER. Cakes cost 17,000–25,000 YER, and a relatives' lunch 300,000 YER. — [Al-Ayyam 2019](https://www.alayyam.info/news/806UD0QK-LI4A8X-E22A)

### Inferences
- Groom-wear shops in Sana'a seem to bundle **sale plus tailoring**: the shopper buys a thobe, coat and shawl and the shop tailors them. Kerbas also calls itself an events contractor, so it may sell packages. The app should let one vendor offer both products and services.
- Bride dresses are mainly a **rental** market, with tiers by condition (used vs. never worn) and accessories included. Deposits can be cash *or collateral* such as gold. An order model needs these fields: rental start and end date, a deposit/collateral record, a condition tier, and delivery/pickup.
- Gold is bought as a set (بدلة/طقم) priced by grams times the daily gold rate. It is often written into the mahr. Prices should therefore be stored as "grams times rate on date" and not as a fixed price.
- Qat is a **volatile, perishable** commodity whose price depends on the day and weather. The app should treat it as a quote at order time, not a catalog price.
- Ordering channels seen: social-media pages (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok), classifieds (OpenSooq), in-store visits, and delivery within the city. All of these imply that orders are arranged on WhatsApp or by phone, but I found no source stating WhatsApp explicitly.

### Gaps
- Kerbas branch count, whether it sells the janbiya or asib, whether it rents anything, and its prices: pages could not be fetched. The owner should verify directly or on Instagram.
- How qat is ordered for guests (المقوّت), bundle units (ربطة, حزمة, كيس) and per-guest amounts: I found no source.
- Sweets and جعالة vendors, bottled-water bulk orders, and invitation-card printing shops (prices, lead times, deposits): I found no sources.
- Recent (2023–2026) dress-rental and gold-set prices in Sana'a in old-rial terms: no sources.

## 2. Total wedding cost and how families pay for it

### Takeaway
Sana'a social estimates in about 2026 put a full urban wedding at roughly **10 million YER (about USD 20,000)**. Large halls alone cost USD 1,200–3,000+, and these are quoted in dollars. Mahr ranges from about 1 million YER under Houthi-era tribal caps to SAR 50,000 in some tribes for out-of-tribe grooms. Families pay through savings, borrowing, selling assets and long-tail debt. Remittances are very large at a national level, but I found no direct source tying them to wedding spending.

### Cited Findings
- **Sana'a total:** gold (الوصلة), mahr, hall rental and banquets total about 10 million YER (about USD 20,000) by social estimates. One father spent about USD 10,000 on his son's wedding, borrowing and selling part of his savings. — [Al-Mahriah, "Poverty & Extravagance", c. Feb 2026 (date inferred from URL)](https://almahriah.net/reports/poverty-extravagance-how-sana-as-weddings-became-a-burden)
- **Sana'a hall prices:** a women's hall charges USD 1,200–2,000 per reception, including about 300 dinners, photography and an all-female band. Wedding "scenarios" are an add-on from USD 150. The "Lavender" hall costs more than USD 3,000. The hall hosts about 6 weddings a month. — [Al-Mahriah](https://almahriah.net/reports/poverty-extravagance-how-sana-as-weddings-became-a-burden)
- **Aden (2019):** some halls charged more than 500,000 YER for about 3.5 hours, with a range of 185,000–550,000 YER, often excluding band and meals. Mahr sometimes exceeded 1,000,000 YER. Grooms took extra jobs, and couples spend years repaying wedding debts. — [Al-Ayyam 2019](https://www.alayyam.info/news/806UD0QK-LI4A8X-E22A)
- Some Aden families hold weddings at home to save money and have asked the authorities to cap hall prices. — [Aden Time](https://www.aden-tm.net/news/333670)
- Some tribes in Sana'a, al-Mahra, Hadramawt and Yafa' demand a mahr of up to SAR 50,000 (about USD 13,300) from out-of-tribe suitors. — [Al-Mahriah, mahr report](https://almahriah.net/local/52075)
- An older source says rural fathers ask about USD 3,000 in mahr and urban fathers about double. — [Al-Watan (via search)](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1155620)
- **Remittances (national):** about 38.6% of GDP in 2024, and inflows fell because of tighter rules in sending countries. — [World Bank Yemen Economic Monitor, Spring 2025](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099822505292530706/pdf/IDU-7009880b-d070-472d-9bf2-5cc72a3fc75d.pdf)
- Saudi Arabia is about 61% of formally recorded inflows. — [MERIP, Sep 2026](https://www.merip.org/2026/09/new-remittances-at-risk-war-sanctions-and-financial-insecurity-in-yemen/)
- Including informal channels, inflows may total as much as USD 10 bn. — [IGC](https://www.theigc.org/blogs/leveraging-remittances-yemen)
- Some families agree to child marriage because the mahr helps them clear debts or buy food and medicine. — [Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/i-only-knew-about-weddings-destitute-yemenis-sell-their-daughters-pay-their-debts)

### Inferences
- Premium vendors such as halls quote in **USD**, while smaller vendors quote in **YER**. The app needs prices in more than one currency.
- Payment is often deferred or paid in instalments, and wedding debt lasts long after the event. Instalment, deposit and balance tracking is a real need.
- Gulf-based expat grooms and relatives probably pay in SAR through exchange companies. This is consistent with the 61% Saudi share, but I found no direct wedding-specific source.

### Gaps
- No survey-based breakdown of a 2024–2025 wedding budget (mahr, gold, hall, lunch, qat, clothes). The 10M YER figure is a journalistic "social estimate".
- No source on رفد or نقوط (guest cash gifts) amounts or practice.
- No source on formal wedding loans (microfinance, banks).

## 3. Currency split: old rial (Sana'a) vs new rial (Aden), 2023–2025

### Takeaway
Two rial zones exist. In Houthi areas (Sana'a), only pre-2017 notes are legal, and the dollar stayed fixed at about **530–540 YER** through 2025. In government areas (Aden), the post-2017 notes collapsed to a record **about 2,905 YER/USD in July 2025**, then recovered to **about 1,600 YER/USD from August 2025** after central-bank action. The same "rial" price therefore means about 3× more value in Sana'a than in Aden.

### Cited Findings
- Notes printed after 2017 by the Aden central bank are not legal tender in the Houthi-controlled north. Sana'a issued its own 200-rial note on 16 Jul 2025, which Aden considers illegal. In January 2025 the rate was about 2,150 YER/USD in government areas and about 530 in Houthi areas. — [Wikipedia: Yemeni rial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_rial)
- Sana'a, 9 Jan 2025: USD buy 535, sell 540. — [Al-Yemen Al-Ghad](https://alyemenalghad.com/economy/%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%84-%D8%A3%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%B5%D8%B1%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%85%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%86%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B5%D9%86/)
- Aden, April 2025: USD 2,399 sell, a historic low at that time; SAR 629 sell / 624 buy. — [South24](https://south24.net/news/newse.php?nid=4577)
- Aden reached an all-time low of 2,905 YER/USD in July 2025. The central bank then fixed SAR at 757 buy / 760 sell and revoked the licences of 28 exchange companies. The rate was about 1,676 by early August 2025. — [South24](https://south24.net/news/newse.php?nid=4816); [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/arab-world/5171280-yemeni-rial-regains-third-its-value-amid-government-push-curb-soaring-prices)
- Aden, 5 Aug 2025: USD 1,617 buy / 1,632 sell. — [Yemen Daily News, 1 Aug 2025](https://ydn.news/?p=90533); [YDN, 24 Aug 2025](https://ydn.news/?p=93234)
- One source claims about 1,065 on 31 Aug 2025; this is a single source and unverified. Analysts question whether the gains will last. — [YGCS](https://www.ygcs.center/en/estimates/article184.html); [World Bank Nov 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/11/17/economic-hardship-deepens-in-yemen)
- In March 2024 the Sana'a central bank ordered all incoming transfers to be paid out in **Saudi riyals**. — [IGC report, 2026](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/YEM-24338%20From%20cash%20to%20capital.pdf) (via search summary)
- The government has barred using foreign currencies in place of the rial in transactions, yet SAR pricing still happens. — Al-Jazeera-family report (via search summary: [aajsa](https://www.aajsa.com/economy/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%B1%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%85-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%87%D8%B8%D8%A9-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%D9%8A-%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%85%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%83-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%AC-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%A9))

### Inferences
- **For the data model,** "YER" alone is ambiguous. Store currency as `YER_SANAA` (old notes) vs `YER_ADEN` (new notes), plus `SAR` and `USD`. Keep each amount in the currency the vendor quoted, and convert only for display, using a dated rate per region.
- Cross-region weddings (for example, a groom in Aden and the bride's family in Sana'a) involve a roughly 3× currency gap and transfer fees. Mahr and gold are likely to be agreed in SAR, USD or gold grams to avoid that gap.
- Prices quoted in Aden in 2024–2025 date quickly because the rate swung from about 2,900 to about 1,600 within weeks.

### Gaps
- No vendor-level source listing which wedding vendors price in SAR/USD beyond halls (USD) and some dress ads (SAR).
- No 2026 rates collected.

## 4. Payment methods: cash, exchange networks, mobile wallets

### Takeaway
Money transfer runs mostly through a large network of exchange companies, most headquartered in Sana'a. Licensed e-wallets exist: M-Floos/Kuraimi, Jawali, Floosak, ONE Cash, Jeeb, YOU (formerly MTN M-Floos) and others. Interoperability between them is limited, and merchant QR payments are used mainly for small purchases in urban shops. I found **no source showing that wedding vendors routinely accept digital payments**. Cash, plus deposits that can be collateral, appears to be the norm.

### Cited Findings
- The number of exchange companies grew from 179 to 415 in Sana'a and from 34 to 91 in Aden between 2014 and 2023. The parallel market took on near-banking roles. — [SEMC, Dec 2025](https://economicmedia.net/en/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Parallel-Market-in-Yemen-Banking-Sector-December-2025.pdf)
- The central bank first licensed e-wallets in 2014, including M-Floos (Kuraimi). A 2020 list added Jawali, We Cash, One Cash and Jeeb. Transfers between wallets of different providers remain limited. — [Sana'a Center, Rethinking Yemen's Economy No.10](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)
- YCASH acquired **Floosak** in late 2020. It offers loans, savings, top-ups, purchases and bill payments. — [YCASH press release, 2021](https://www.findevgateway.org/sites/default/files/news/submissions/83831/YCASH%20New%20Partnerships%20with%20Humanitarian%20Agencies%20Deploying%20Humanitarian%20Transfers%20Platform%20and%20National%20Digital%20Wallet-V1-25-4-2021.pdf)
- M-Floos use by women is rising, especially in rural areas with few banks. — [ResearchGate, Kuraimi study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395188675_Yemeni_Women's_Financial_Inclusion_The_Case_of_Al-Kuraimi_Islamic_Bank)
- **ONE Cash** advertises payment points nationwide, merchant payments, and transfers to Jawali and Floosak. This is vendor marketing. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.one.onecustomer)
- The payment ecosystem is mainly telecom wallets, and ONE Cash and YOU Wallet cover small QR payments in urban shops. This is a vendor blog, low reliability. — [Litashared](https://www.litashared.com/blog/detail/index172.html)
- Aden revoked the licences of six major Sana'a banks in 2024 and later reversed. Licensing conflicts between Aden and Sana'a disrupt transfers. — [World Bank YEM Spring 2025](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099822505292530706/pdf/IDU-7009880b-d070-472d-9bf2-5cc72a3fc75d.pdf); [Sana'a Center Yemen Review Q2 2024](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2024/22888)
- Zakat Authority mass-wedding grants were paid out in person at the Tahrir post office in Sana'a. — [Al-Haqiqah, 2024](https://www.alhagigah.net/%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%AA-%D8%B4%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A7-%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%B5%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D9%88%D9%85%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%AC%D9%87%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD)

### Inferences
- For a first version, the app should model payments as **records**: method (cash, exchange transfer, wallet name), reference number, currency and region. It should not try to process payments, because the wallets don't work with each other and are split between Aden and Sana'a.
- Deposits paid by transfer through exchange networks (Kuraimi, Al-Najm, Al-Amqi, etc.) to vendors in other cities are plausible, especially from expats, but I found no wedding-specific source.

### Gaps
- No source on Al-Najm or Al-Amqi transfer usage for weddings, or on how many wedding vendors accept Jawali, Floosak, ONE Cash or Cash. No current wallet user counts.

## 5. Seasons and days of the week

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin. Al-Ayyam described a "wedding season" in **November 2019** in Aden, with intense gold buying. Al-Araby reports that many Sana'a families marry **during Eid al-Adha**, partly because qat is cheaper then. I found no sources on the post-Eid al-Fitr (Shawwal) peak, summer peaks, a Ramadan or Muharram lull, or preferred weekdays.

### Cited Findings
- Al-Ayyam (18 Nov 2019) says it was "currently wedding season" in Aden, with many weddings and active gold buying. — [Al-Ayyam](https://www.alayyam.info/news/806UD0QK-LI4A8X-E22A)
- A Sana'a car-decoration shop owner said many residents hold weddings during Eid al-Adha, citing among other reasons lower qat prices then. The article is undated. — [Al-Araby, "موسم الأعراس في اليمن بلا أعراس"](https://www.alaraby.co.uk/%D9%85%D9%88%D8%B3%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B9%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B3-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%A7-%D8%A3%D8%B9%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B3) (search snippet; page returned 403)
- Qat prices spike in winter because of frost. — [Al-Jarida](https://www.aljarida.com/article/90857)
- One Sana'a hall hosts about 6 weddings a month. — [Al-Mahriah](https://almahriah.net/reports/poverty-extravagance-how-sana-as-weddings-became-a-burden)
- A Houthi-area tribal pact (Bani al-Harith, Sana'a) bans **night-time weddings**, and others restrict end times. — [More to Her Story](https://www.moretoherstory.com/stories/in-yemen-tribal-decrees-are-tightening-their-grip-on-women-as-inequality-deepens)
- The Zakat Authority's 4th mass wedding was launched in **July 2024** (summer). — [Masa Press, 2 Jul 2024](https://masa-press.net/2024/07/02/%D9%87%D9%8A%D8%A6%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D9%83%D8%A7%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B5%D9%86%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%AA%D8%B7%D9%84%D9%82-%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D8%B9%D8%B1%D8%B3-%D8%AC%D9%85%D8%A7/)

### Inferences
- Peaks follow the Hijri calendar and move about 11 days a year, so availability and pricing should be keyed to Hijri dates and Eids, not fixed Gregorian months.
- Qat cost is seasonal and higher in winter, which affects the overall budget.
- Rules from local pacts, such as bans on night weddings or music, can limit time slots by area.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence for a Shawwal peak, a summer or expat-return peak, Ramadan or Muharram lulls, or preferred weekdays (Thursday or Friday nights are commonly assumed but unsourced).

## 6. Government and community initiatives limiting wedding costs

### Takeaway
In Houthi areas, more than **70 tribal "pacts"** since 2019 cap mahr, for example **1,000,000 YER (about USD 1,900)** in Wasab al-Ali, Dhamar. Many also ban music, DJs and night weddings, and fines reach 300,000 YER. A pact attributed to Yahya al-Qasimi caps mahr at **800,000 YER**. The Houthi-run **Zakat Authority's mass weddings** have funded 7,200, then 10,044, then 11,000 couples; the fourth, in 2024, cost 4.4 bn YER in total. Mass weddings by NGOs go back to the mid-1990s.

### Cited Findings
- More than 70 tribal agreements across 70 tribes in Houthi areas since 2019, with a sharp increase recently. The Wasab al-Ali (Dhamar) pact caps mahr at 1M YER (about USD 1,900) and restricts women's phones, travel and internet use. An Amran pact bans songs, keyboards, DJs and loudspeakers, except Houthi zawamil, with a fine of 300,000 YER (about USD 600). A Bani al-Harith (Sana'a) pact signed by Sheikh Yahya Jameel bans singing and night weddings. Critics call the pacts tools of control, and Houthi supervisors co-sign them. ### Inferences
- Local rules (mahr caps, music bans, no night weddings, fines) vary by district or tribe and directly affect which vendors can operate, such as DJs, bands and late halls. An app operating in Houthi areas may need region-level rules or flags on vendor categories.
- Mass-wedding grants (about 400,000–850,000 YER per couple) are far below the about 10M YER cost of a full urban wedding, so they cover only a basic package.

### Gaps
- No independent verification of how strictly the caps are enforced, and no equivalent initiatives found in government-held (Aden) areas.
- Nothing found on a 2025 or 2026 Zakat mass wedding.
